# The `data` layer

State on 2026-09-27. This document explains how a provider's answer becomes an
observation a decision could have known, and how to add an instrument or a
source. The summary is in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md) §5, the formulas
in [FORMULAS.md](FORMULAS.md) §5, and corporate actions in
[CORPORATE_ACTIONS.md](CORPORATE_ACTIONS.md).

The layer must preserve three things: **the original value, its provenance and
the instant it became available**. Filling a hole with the last value makes a
nice chart, but it invents a data point. Mixing a raw price and an adjusted
price creates a return that never existed.

## 1. The declared instruments

`market_data/metadata/instruments.toml`:

| Id | Nature | Main source (check) | Calendar | Tradable | Since | `history_basis` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| `ETF_SP500_PEA` | accumulating ETF, EUR, whole units | Yahoo `ESE.PA` (Euronext) | XPAR | yes | 2014-03-18 | `ASSUMED_UNREVISED` |
| `ETF_WORLD` | accumulating ETF, EUR, whole units | Yahoo `CW8.PA` (Euronext) | XPAR | yes | 2018-04-18 | `ASSUMED_UNREVISED` |
| `VIX` | volatility level, no currency | FRED `VIXCLS` | published 16:15 New York | no | 1990-01-02 | `ASSUMED_UNREVISED` |

| `ECB_EURUSD` | exchange rate, USD per EUR | ECB `EXR.D.USD.EUR.SP00.A` | published the same day | no | 1999-01-04 | `ASSUMED_UNREVISED` |

Universes (`universes.toml`, dated members): `ROTATION_2` contains the two
ETFs, `ETF_SP500_PEA` from 2018-04-18. `MACRO_3` contains the three published
series, which are read as information and never traded.

Units: a rate of 4.25 means 4.25% a year, not 425%. EURUSD is a number of
dollars per euro. The VIX is an index computed from options: it is neither a
return nor a unit one can hold. An ECB fixing is not an execution price.

## 2. The sources and their limits

| Source | Serves | Declared limits |
|---|---|---|
| Yahoo (`sources/yahoo.py`) | daily bars, splits and dividends (separate table) | with `auto_adjust=False`, prices before a split are still adjusted, so **a stock or an ETF with a split in its history is refused**; no announcement or payment date; nothing for a delisted security |
| Euronext (`sources/euronext.py`) | a second series of bars, for the cross-check | a rolling window of 730 days (`EURONEXT_WINDOW`, a convention of the adapter, not a promise of the provider); the request is adapted after `ProviderRangeUnavailable` |
| FRED (`sources/fred.py`) | published levels | the **latest version** of the series, hence restated |
| ALFRED (`sources/alfred.py`) | the versions (*vintages*) of a FRED series | implemented, but no instrument uses it |
| ECB (`sources/ecb.py`) | reference rates | a fixing, not OHLC bars |

Every provider error inherits from `ProviderError` (`ProviderUnavailable`,
`ProviderRateLimited`, `ProviderResponseError`, …). A check source that does
not answer becomes a warning, and ingestion goes on. A `TypeError` in our own
reading code stops everything.

**Calendars** (`metadata/calendars/`, generated by
`scripts/generate_calendars.py` from `exchange_calendars`, then checked session
by session): XNYS from 1990 to the end of 2027, XPAR from 2002 to the end of
2027, XETR (Xetra, for the four funds of SA7 and SA8) from 2014 to the end of
2027. Outside them, one gets `CalendarCoverageError`. A weekday is never
assumed to be a session. `scripts/check_calendar_coverage.py` fails when fewer
than 90 days of coverage remain.

## 3. The four dates

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `session_date` / `observation_date` | the day the data point **describes** (`date`) |
| `open_available_at_utc`, `close_available_at_utc` / `available_at_utc` | the instant from which the reader **serves** it |
| `retrieved_at_utc` | the instant of the download |
| `vintage_date` | the publication version of a revised series |

The reader enforces `available ≤ decision instant`. A bar has **two**
instants: the OPEN is available at the open, while HIGH, LOW, CLOSE and VOLUME
are available at the close (`schemas.AVAILABILITY_COLUMN`). Knowing the
session's high before its end would be reading the future. A published level
is available according to its `PublicationRule`: time, time zone and offset in
sessions of a declared calendar.

## 4. The canonical schemas (`data/schemas.py`)

Every timestamp is in UTC, to the microsecond. Prices are `float64` in the
instrument's currency, and volume is a number of units traded.

- `BARS_SCHEMA`: identifier, date, OHLCV, both availabilities, `source`,
  `source_fetch_id`.
- `CHECKED_BARS_SCHEMA`: the bar retained, plus `check_status`,
  `checked_sources`, `checked_fetch_ids`, `conflicting_fields`,
  `unconfirmed_fields`, `max_price_rel_diff` and `max_volume_rel_diff`. **It is
  what strategies read.**
- `LEVELS_SCHEMA`: date, value, availability, provenance.
- `VINTAGES_SCHEMA`: adds `vintage_date` and `withdrawn`. A value withdrawn by
  a later publication is a **withdrawal row**. If it were deleted, the old
  value would come back.
- `CORPORATE_ACTIONS_SCHEMA`: type, ex-date, value, availability, provenance.

## 5. Cleaning without inventing

1. **Normalise** (`normalizer.py`). The provider's columns become the
   canonical fields, and the dates become sessions of the venue. `bars_frame`
   dates each field and sets aside three cases, which it names in
   `RejectedRows`:
   - `non_session`: a day the venue held no session;
   - `unlisted`: a day outside the listing period. Yahoo serves 74 flat net
     asset values of CW8 before its listing;
   - `unpublished`: a bar downloaded before its close, which is therefore not
     final.
2. **Validate** (`validator.py`). There are two levels:
   - `ERROR` blocks the promotion: a zero or negative price, a negative volume,
     an inconsistent OHLC, a duplicate, an invalid action;
   - `WARNING` lets it through. **An extreme move is a warning, not an
     error**: deleting a real crash would flatter the strategy.
3. **Correct what must be corrected, without repairing anything.** A
   `BarCorrection` (`bar_corrections.toml`) **deletes** one precise bar and
   gives its reason. It never manufactures a replacement price. If the defect
   it targets has disappeared from a new answer, the correction is refused and
   must be reviewed. A `KnownGap` (`known_gaps.toml`) explains a hole without
   filling it.

## 6. Reconciling two sources (`crosscheck.py`)

The gap `δ` is defined in [FORMULAS.md](FORMULAS.md) §5, the tolerances per
field in `crosscheck.toml`.

- A field whose gap exceeds its tolerance makes the row `CONFLICT`. The reader
  **blocks that field only**: a contested low does not hide a concordant
  close.
- Fewer than two finite values for a field makes it **unconfirmed**, not an
  agreement.
- The retained series contains the dates of the main source. A date that only
  the check source has is reported, but it is not served.
- `REVIEWED` (`conflict_reviews.toml`) means that a review chose a source for
  the exact values it examined. **It is not a consensus.** If the values
  change, the review no longer applies.

## 7. Revisions and history

Providers rewrite the past. By default, **the first value stored remains the
canonical value**. `detect_revisions` logs every difference, and
`merge_with_policy` adds the new dates. It applies an old change only if its
exact identity (source, instrument, date, field, old and new value) appears in
`accepted_revisions.toml`. `scripts/accept_revision.py` prepares the block to
review. The resulting bar is validated again: accepting a single field can make
the OHLC inconsistent.

Each instrument declares what its history is, with `history_basis` and
`history_note`, both mandatory in the TOML:

| `HistoryBasis` | What can be claimed |
|---|---|
| `ASSUMED_UNREVISED` | the values served today are assumed to be those of the past (an exchange close, a fixing published once); `history_note` says why |
| `RESTATED` | the series contains corrections made after the decisions: a backtest that reads it has a look-ahead it must declare (`US10Y`) |
| `ARCHIVED_VINTAGES` | each value as it was published; only for a series read by versions, with `vintage_policy = AS_OF_DECISION` |

A series read by versions also declares its `VintagePolicy`. `AS_OF_DECISION`
gives the last version the decision could see. `PINNED` freezes a single
version for the whole study: it is reproducible, but not faithful to what each
date knew.

Timestamping the fields protects the reading. It does not turn a restated
series into an archive of publications. Reviews and corrections are research
configuration: they are not dated.

## 8. Storing and replaying (`repository.py`, `updater.py`)

- `raw/` is immutable: each download is archived with a JSON manifest,
  including those that were **refused**.
- `clean/` is the derived layer. A promotion writes the checked bars, the
  revisions, the validation issues and `applied_fetches.parquet`, **in a single
  transaction**: the files are prepared under `.pending/`, a SHA-256 manifest
  is written, then everything is published under an exclusive lock. A recovery
  completes a publication already decided or throws away an unfinished
  preparation.
- The lock (`market_data/.lock`, `flock`, POSIX only) is shared for a run and
  exclusive for a write. A concurrent writer receives `StoreBusy`.
- Limit: it is **not** an atomic snapshot at the file-system level. During the
  few microseconds of the renames, a reader that did not take the lock could
  see an old file and a new one.
- `rebuild_clean` (`--rebuild`) replays **only the downloads listed in
  `applied_fetches.parquet`**, since `raw/` also contains refusals. The full
  rebuild is a single transaction. It fails without writing anything if a
  download of the journal is missing from `raw/`, or if a replay no longer
  passes.

> **Never delete `clean/` to "start from scratch".** The journal
> `applied_fetches.parquet` is inside it. Without it, the replay no longer
> knows which downloads had been promoted, and rebuilds an empty store. To
> rebuild, run `--rebuild` on an intact `clean/`.

## 9. Reading (`reader.py`)

`MarketDataReader.at(instant)` returns a frozen `PointInTimeReader`. Its
methods take no date beyond that instant. `_available_rows` applies
availability, conflicts and versions, then `_observe` adds a status (`OK`,
`STALE`, `NOT_LISTED`, `MISSING`) and an age counted in sessions of the
reference calendar. A hole is never filled by carrying a price forward.

The complete sequence of an update:

1. `scripts/update_market_data.py:build_updater` loads the TOML files, the
   adapters (`SOURCES`) and the normalisers (`NORMALIZERS`).
2. `update` or `archive_history` downloads. `save_raw` archives each answer.
3. The normalisers and corrections apply, then the validation.
4. If nothing blocks, the promotion merges, checks the sources against each
   other and writes in one transaction.
5. During a backtest, `MarketDataReader.at` provides the dated reader to the
   signals and to the engine.

## 10. Adding an instrument from an existing source

1. **Identify the instrument precisely**: name, ISIN, quotation currency,
   symbol at the provider, listing dates, distribution policy. Tell apart the
   share classes of a single fund.
2. **Declare it** in `instruments.toml` by copying a comparable instrument:
   `history_basis` and `history_note` are mandatory, and so is
   `distribution_policy` for a fund. `tradable = true` requires an execution
   model that fits (the same currency as the book, `quantity_step` lots): that
   a price can be downloaded is not enough.
3. For a **bar**, give the exact calendar of the venue. For a **level**, give
   the publication time, time zone and offset. For versions that are really
   archived, use ALFRED with `AS_OF_DECISION`.
4. **Add it to a dated universe** in `universes.toml`. A macroeconomic
   indicator is requested as information (`SignalRequest`); it does not enter
   the traded universe.
5. **Download, then check**:

   ```bash
   uv run python scripts/update_market_data.py --archive --only ETF_WORLD   # the whole declared history
   uv run python scripts/update_market_data.py --coverage --only ETF_WORLD  # what is missing, without downloading
   uv run python scripts/update_market_data.py --only ETF_WORLD             # extend the series
   uv run python scripts/update_market_data.py --rebuild --only ETF_WORLD   # replay raw/, without network
   ```

   Read the report: bounds, holes, OHLC, warnings, conflicts, availability.
   Check a few sessions by hand against the venue's website. A conflict is
   settled in `conflict_reviews.toml`, a hole is explained in `known_gaps.toml`,
   and nothing is corrected silently.

## 11. Adding a source

Two classes, and the adapter keeps **all** of the provider's quirks:

```python
"""A local source: one CSV per instrument, raw prices, no corporate action feed."""

from __future__ import annotations

from datetime import UTC, date, datetime
from pathlib import Path

import pandas as pd

from quant_backtester.data.calendars import TradingCalendar
from quant_backtester.data.instruments import Instrument
from quant_backtester.data.normalizer import NormalizedData, bars_frame
from quant_backtester.data.sources.base import RawDownload, make_fetch_id


class CsvSource:
    """Read ``<directory>/<source_symbol>.csv``: date,open,high,low,close,volume."""

    source_id = "LOCAL_CSV"

    def __init__(self, directory: Path) -> None:
        self.directory = directory

    def available_from(self, instrument: Instrument) -> date | None:
        return None  # no declared window

    def download(self, instrument: Instrument, start: date, end: date) -> RawDownload:
        if start > end:
            raise ValueError(f"inverted period: {start} > {end}")
        path = self.directory / f"{instrument.source_symbol}.csv"
        frame = pd.read_csv(path, dtype={"date": str})
        days = frame["date"].map(date.fromisoformat)
        now = datetime.now(UTC)  # the instant of the download: it is a data point
        return RawDownload(
            instrument_id=instrument.id,
            source=self.source_id,
            fetch_id=make_fetch_id(now),
            retrieved_at_utc=now,
            frame=frame.loc[days.between(start, end)].reset_index(drop=True),
            request={"path": str(path), "start": str(start), "end_inclusive": str(end)},
        )

    def download_corporate_actions(
        self, instrument: Instrument, start: date, end: date
    ) -> RawDownload | None:
        return None  # no corporate action feed: only for a series without events


class CsvNormalizer:
    source_id = "LOCAL_CSV"

    def normalize(
        self, instrument: Instrument, download: RawDownload, calendar: TradingCalendar | None = None
    ) -> NormalizedData:
        if calendar is None:
            raise ValueError(f"{instrument.id}: a bar needs its calendar")
        frame = download.frame
        days = [date.fromisoformat(day) for day in frame["date"]]
        values = {
            field: [float(value) for value in frame[field]]
            for field in ("open", "high", "low", "close", "volume")
        }
        bars, rejected = bars_frame(instrument, download, calendar, days, values)
        return NormalizedData(bars=bars, rejected=rejected)
```

The example leaves out the numpy docstrings to stay short. They remain
mandatory in delivered code.

To plug it in:

- add `"LOCAL_CSV": lambda: CsvSource(Path("my_csvs"))` to `SOURCES` in
  `scripts/update_market_data.py`;
- add `CsvNormalizer()` to `NORMALIZERS` in `data/normalizer.py`;
- declare `primary_source = "LOCAL_CSV"`, with `source_symbol` equal to the
  file name without its extension.

`bars_frame` dates the fields and sets aside weekends, days outside the
listing and bars not yet published. It refuses a missing field or a duplicate
session. The adapter only has to produce session dates and five columns of
numbers. For an Excel file, only the reading changes. A source without a
corporate action feed only suits a series checked to have no events.
Otherwise, its corporate action feed must be written before it is used.

**Minimum tests for a new source** (on the model of
`tests/data/test_normalizer.py`, `test_reader.py`, `test_updater.py`, without
network, on a recorded answer):

- three known sessions: open, close and exact UTC instants;
- a duplicate, a weekend date, a close outside `[low, high]` and a row
  downloaded before the close: each gives an explicit diagnostic;
- a reading just before the open, just after, then after the close;
- **the look-ahead guard**: adding a future row changes no earlier reading;
- a real `@pytest.mark.network` test, run separately
  (`uv run pytest -m network`).
